This repository will contain files pertaining to the second experiment from my PhD.

**As of the moment I'm writing this (november 8, 2026) I'm working on the codification manual that can be viewed in the corresponding folder.**

This repository hosts data, analytical scripts, codification manuals and code for an empirical study investigating how humorous framing (memes) in political messaging influences small-group political conversation dynamics, perspective-taking, and future political engagement.
Given ethical considerations, no transcription nor audiovisual media created in the project will be uploade (see note be)

- Political discussions often carry a high perceived seriousness that deters individuals from engaging with opposing viewpoints. This study examines whether introducing humorous cues creates a more playful and open conversational climate, ultimately lowering barriers to political discourse among ideologically diverse individuals.

- Objectives:
  - Discursive Engagement: Test whether humorous framing leads to more ambivalent evaluations, greater consideration of divergent perspectives, and longer, more engaged discussions.
  - Conversational Outcomes: Examine how the form and flow of small-group interactions influence perceived agreement, sense of consensus, and willingness to discuss politics with opposing views in the future. 

The study will use Systemic Functional Linguistics coding (Schleppegrell & Oteíza, 2023), later quantified through Epistemic Network Analysis (Weiler et al., 2022), to examine whether humorous cues prompt more ambivalent evaluations, foster the consideration of divergent perspectives, and sustain prolonged discursive engagement. 
Analysis of the form and flow of the conversation will also be conducted in order to account for their role on the experience of agreement, sense of consensus and overall willingness to engage in political conversation with people from opposing views (D’Amore et al., 2026; Koudenburg et al., 2024).
